# China Payment, Parcel & Security Rules — verified facts, with sources and check dates

A reference for the everyday-China rules foreign visitors most often get wrong: what a QR payment can cost in a day, what a foreign card is limited to, what you may post and what is banned, what you may carry onto a plane, and the real-name rules behind a SIM card.

Every figure below carries **the official source that states it** and **the date it was last checked**. If you copy a number anywhere, copy the check date with it — that is what makes the number verifiable at all.

- `facts.json` — the same 24 entries, machine-readable
- The parent guide for these rules: <https://jiunotes.top/en/life/>
- How entries are kept current: see [Maintenance](#maintenance)

## Why this exists

English-language guides to daily life in China are usually written once, from memory, and then age silently. QR-code limits get quoted from a rule that changed years ago. Parcel rules and airport rules are described vaguely because nobody checks the Chinese regulation. This file ties each English sentence to the official Chinese source and says when it was last looked at.

The rules themselves are not secret — the central bank, the postal service, the civil aviation administration and the government portals publish them, in Chinese. The gap is that almost nobody ties the English sentence back to the Chinese source, or says when they last looked. This file exists to close that gap.

**Scope, and a note on honesty.** These are published rules, limits and fee schedules. They are not travel advice, they are not a booking or payment service, and this is not an official publication. Where a rule could not be confirmed against an official page, it is not listed here — a shorter list beats a guessed number.

## What is *not* in this file

- **Live prices or fees for a specific item on a specific day.** Those move with the market and are only authoritative inside the app at the moment of purchase.
- **Visa, entry and health rules.** Different authority, different change cadence; conflating them is how travel content goes wrong.
- **Anything we could not verify.** If no official Chinese page states it, it is not here.

## Maintenance

This dataset is not written once and abandoned:

- Every source URL is re-fetched and re-checked **monthly**. When a page's content changes, the entry is flagged for a human re-check rather than silently updated.
- Each entry carries a `recheck_by` date in `facts.json`. Entries approaching that date surface as a re-check task.
- If a figure changes, its `checked_on` date changes with it. A number is never quietly swapped while the old date stays in place.

## Citation

Media, forums, wikis, course material and other guides may cite this table with attribution:

> Jiu's Sharing Station, "China Payment, Parcel & Security Rules", checked 2026-11-10, https://jiunotes.top/en/life/

Two requests, both about accuracy rather than credit:

1. **Carry the check date with the figure.** A limit without a date is not information — it is a rumor with a number attached.
2. **For anything time-sensitive, link the official source we list, not this file.** Our row is a pointer; the Chinese source is the authority.

## Machine-readable mirror & schema

`facts.json` is also served through a CDN, which helps when raw.githubusercontent.com is slow or blocked:

- <https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fxlcj/china-payment-logistics-facts@main/facts.json>

Field names differ between our repositories. This repo uses `claim_en` and a `source` object with `name` and `url`; `china-food-rules-facts` and `china-festival-calendar-facts` use a flat `claim` / `source_url` pair. Every entry in every repo carries the same three essentials: the claim, the official source URL, and the check date. See `gaps.md` for what we could not verify and what we deliberately left out.

The official source URL in each row remains the authority. The CDN is only a copy of `facts.json`.
